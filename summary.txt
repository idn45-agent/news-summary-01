World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks ended in Abu Dhabi with agreement in principle on a large prisoner-of-war exchange, called the war's biggest by envoy Jared Kushner, but no ceasefire or territorial deal, the Associated Press reported. Iran security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran awaited a US answer to its seven conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, Al Jazeera reported; the IRGC claimed three tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines, and monitors counted at least 37 attacks near the strait in November, a wartime monthly high. The US Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 plots in the US and Europe, including a September plot against US aircraft at RAF Fairford, the BBC reported. Leaders opened the COP31 summit in Antalya, Turkey, where President Erdogan pushed a "35 by 35" electrification goal and UN chief Antonio Guterres urged faster action, with the US absent again, Reuters reported. Ebola cases in DR Congo passed 11,000: 11,042 confirmed cases and at least 5,387 deaths, in a Bundibugyo-strain outbreak now the second largest on record, Reuters reported. The Associated Press called Mississippi's Senate race for Democrat Scott Colom over Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith by 4,669 votes; he will be the state's first Black senator since 1881. The US House voted 211-204 to refer an impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, shelving it, the BBC reported. US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and core prices 0.3%, both above forecasts, strengthening expectations of a December Fed rate hike, CNBC reported. President Bukele said Salvadoran and Haitian forces destroyed Haiti's 400 Mawozo gang headquarters near Port-au-Prince; its leader survived, and reported deaths of about 20 were unconfirmed, AFP reported. In Seongnam, South Korea, a bus ploughed into 32 vehicles, injuring 57 people, nine seriously, Reuters reported.
